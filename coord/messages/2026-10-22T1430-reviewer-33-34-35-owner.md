## 34: Mechanics must make sense; ask "why would that happen?"
The owner: "injuries like a hurt ankle shouldn't get worse the longer it takes to get to the doctor. How would it get worse? Only if the character tried to keep walking on it, sure, but they're not exactly bleeding out. We need to always ask logical questions about mechanics like this."

I found it in data: `services.json` "late": "The ambulance took {min} minutes. {name} got worse while waiting." It's applied to a twisted ankle (my family run: Kenneth, d37, a 21-minute ambulance, a 700-coin bill).

**Please:**
1. **Fix the case:** a sprain or minor injury doesn't worsen with waiting. It worsens only if the folk keeps walking or working on it (then a longer recovery), and it doesn't need an ambulance at all. Home care, the clinic, or a taxi or lift to the clinic; an ambulance only for serious injuries.
2. **Worsening and delay rules by cause:** only for things that really get worse with time: bleeding, burns, heart attack, stroke, choking, an overdose, hypothermia, heatstroke, poisoning, a serious fall, labour complications. Each has a reason in data ("bleeding: worsens per minute without first aid").
3. **A logic audit** of every mechanic added (incidents 02–07b, 12, 18, 21, 23, 14, 29), using one question per rule: "Why would this happen? Would a real person expect it?" Find rules that don't make sense, and write them up as a short table: rule, problem, fix. Examples to check:

## 35: Owner decision on how the game is played. Please reply with a design before building.
The owner: "player plays as one character at a time, until that character dies. **No autonomous behaviour at all** for that character. The game will be working your way through a world that grows and reacts to you as you go, throwing challenges and curveballs at you based on difficulty."

So:
- **One character:** the player controls one folk, not the household. The rest of the household and the town are AI-driven, autonomous NPCs that react to the player (they're already the "townies plus household" sim).
- **Zero autonomy for the player's character:** nothing happens unless the player orders it (no need-driven picks, no idle fillers, no 14b outings, no 32c plans or reactions for them).
- **Succession:** play continues until that character dies; then the heir flow from task 15 picks the next character, a child or relative (the owner's earlier heir rule still applies).
- **The world reacts and grows** with the player: the storyteller (29) aimed at the player's character and their circle, challenges scaled by difficulty.

**Questions for your design reply** (the owner and I will decide; I'll give my recommendations to the owner):
1. **Survival without autonomy:** what happens if the player leaves the character alone? Options: they stand still and needs fall, and they can faint, wet themselves or collapse (with warnings); or a tiny set of reflexes (flee a fire, wake to a smoke alarm, stop a choking fit). Which reflexes are safe to keep without breaking "no autonomy"?
2. **Player-made routines:** let the player set their own rules ("go to bed at 23:00", "eat when hungry below 30", "go to work on time") as player orders, not AI. Is that within the owner's rule? I think yes (it's the player's choice), but the owner decides.
3. **Pacing:** with one character to micromanage, the needs decay, task lengths and the time skip (sleep, work, travel) need tuning so it isn't tedious. Propose numbers.
4. **The household:** other family members act on their own and can be asked to do things (ask your partner to cook, tell the kids to do homework), with an obey chance by relationship, age and traits. Should the player switch character at all before death? (The owner says no.)
5. **Co-op:** each player is one character, which fits; time skip when all players sleep or work stays.
6. **UI:** the camera follows the character, and the panels centre on them (needs, goals, relationships), with the household shown as other people.
7. **What changes in existing systems:** list them (autonomy gates, StoryPacing, 14b, 32c, homemaker, AutoFurnish (32f) for the player's own household, commute, BabyCare pulling the player, tests that assume an autonomous player household, the hash baseline).
8. **The difficulty ladder:** how the storyteller, money, needs decay and event severity scale with Easy, Normal and Hard.

Please post the design, an effort estimate and what breaks; build after the owner decides.
