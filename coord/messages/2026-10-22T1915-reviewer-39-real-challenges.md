**39: Real challenges with stakes (owner request).** The owner, after the spicy run: "That wasn't spicy enough, no real challenges happened."

**The numbers agree.** On the maximum Custom settings, over 109 days:
- 3 `[bad]` toasts and 13 warnings were about Zack himself, out of 16 bad toasts town-wide;
- his coins never went below 2,279;
- every problem was a one-off toast that the autonomy or a bill quietly absorbed: a break-in with no felt loss, a 600-coin identity theft against a 4,000-coin cushion, a neighbour feud that just faded, flu and a cold that passed.

The spice went into frequency in the town, not into **stakes for the player**. Turning the sliders to max can't fix that. It needs a design change, and it fits 36 (one character, starting poor).

**What "a real challenge" means:** something that threatens what the player cares about (money, home, job, health, relationships, freedom, reputation), lasts long enough to need a response, gets worse if ignored, and has more than one way out, each with a cost. Please design and propose, with a cause line for each (34 rule):

1. **Aim the storyteller at the player:** `pawn_focus` (the 35 design, section 8) and a share of the budget that's only for the player's own life. On Chaotic or Custom-max, at least one medium or big personal challenge per game week, and no more than 3 quiet days in a row for the player.
2. **Pressure that builds** (with the 36 economy, where rent and bills are real):
   - rent due weekly, with a missed-payment warning, a late fee, then an eviction notice, then a deadline;
   - debt, with a loan shark as a choice on hard modes;
   - the job at risk (a bad review, probation, a rival trying to get you fired, the business closing);
   - an injury that stops you working for days while bills keep coming.
3. **Escalating arcs aimed at the player** (`storyteller_arcs`, built on what's there):
   - a feud that grows from a complaint to rumours, vandalism, a court case and a fight;
   - a partner's crisis (infidelity, a break-up threat, an ultimatum);
   - being wrongly accused (an arrest, a court case, clearing your name);
   - a rival for your job or your partner;
   - a stalker or obsessive admirer (non-sexual, safety-focused, from the 29 rules);
   - a friend in trouble asking for a big favour or money;
   - a family secret that changes things (the Lark Hooper arc should end with something real: a sibling, debt or an inheritance with conditions);
   - an addiction-style habit only as a non-graphic mood or health spiral, if at all (ask the owner first).
4. **Consequences that stick:**
   - a break-in takes named objects you have to replace;
   - a fire at your own home makes a room unusable until it's repaired;
   - hospital stays cost real money and lost shifts;
   - reputation follows you (gossip changes how townies greet you, job offers and prices);
   - court can end in a fine you can't pay, community service clashing with work, or jail.
5. **Choices with costs:** most challenges pop a decision (pay, fight, hide, ask for help, cut corners or illegal options from 21/23), and the outcome is shown and remembered. With no autonomy (36) the player must act; ignoring it is also a choice, and the game shows what that costs.
6. **Difficulty sets the stakes, not just the count:** Easy has soft landings (a shelter bed, a charity hand-out); Hard has eviction to the street, debt collectors and real jail time. The hard rarity rules stay (murder, serial killers, child abuse, disasters capped; no trafficking, no sexual content).
7. **Measure it with a test:** a `pawn_bot` on Normal and Hard, 5 random seeds × 60 days. Report per player-week: personal challenges started, challenges resolved by a choice, the lowest coins as a share of a week's costs, days under threat of eviction or job loss. Targets: Normal at least 1 personal challenge a week, and at least 2 times in 60 days where the bot was close to losing its home or job; Hard more often. Plus a run where the bot ignores everything and loses its home by about week 3.

Please post the design and an estimate. It probably merges into the 36 stages (after S1 and S2), so say where it fits.
