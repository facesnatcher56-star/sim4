**09: A full legal system.** The owner confirmed that player Folk **can go to jail**. They also want a real legal process to decide it, not an automatic sentence. Build it after 04 (crime) and for everyone in town (task 08), with townies resolved statistically when far.

**1. Investigation and arrest.**
- Crimes leave **evidence** (witnesses, items found on the suspect, the motive from relationships and grudges, the alibi from where they were at the time, reputation).
- Police build a case over days. Arrest needs enough evidence, and the wrong person can be arrested if the evidence points at them. A **wrongful conviction** is possible but rare.

**2. Charges, bail, lawyers.**
- **Charges:** theft, burglary, assault, robbery, arson, manslaughter (accidental death, for example a fire caused by negligence) and murder, each with a severity.
- **Bail:** pay coins to go home until the trial, or stay in jail.
- **Lawyer:** a public defender for free (weak), or hire a private lawyer for coins (better). Townie lawyers have skill levels.

**3. Trial.**
- A trial date a few days later. A courthouse venue (rabbit hole or a small lot) with a judge and a jury of townies.
- The verdict is a scored roll from evidence strength, witness testimony (relationships matter: friends defend you, enemies testify against you), lawyer skill vs prosecutor, the defendant's charisma or reputation, and the jury's opinion of them.
- **Plea deal option:** plead guilty for a lighter sentence.
- Player Folk can **attend as witnesses** and testify for or against someone (a social interaction that affects the outcome). Lying on the stand is possible, with a perjury risk.
- **Verdicts:** not guilty / guilty / mistrial. Players see a short result screen and the Chronicle and newspaper report it.

**4. Sentences.**
- A fine, community service (a daily task for a few days), probation (any new crime gets punished harder), jail for days, or prison for the longest stretch.
- Serious murder sentences can outlast the rest of the playthrough. Make that clear in the warning prompt.

**5. Prison.**
- A venue/rabbit hole.
- **Behaviour:** good behaviour can earn early parole, while fights inside add time.
- **Visits:** family and friends can visit, which keeps relationships from decaying.
- **On release:** a criminal record, job restrictions, a reputation drop, and a "fresh start" goal.

**6. Appeals and pardons.** A rare chance to appeal with new evidence, plus exonerations that make the news.

**7. Civil cases** (lighter):
- Lawsuits after injuries on someone's property (accidents from task 05).
- Divorce settlements and child custody (ties into the existing divorce code).
- Inheritance disputes.
- Small claims for damaged or stolen property.

**8. Careers:** police officer, detective, lawyer and judge as new careers, with daily tasks tied to real cases in town. A detective can actually solve the town's cases.

**Notifications:** pop-ups only when the household is involved (arrested, testifying, a family member on trial); everything else goes to the Chronicle and the paper. Tests: forced cases with fixed evidence that give the expected verdicts, plus a statistical slow test over many cases checking conviction rates (guilty with strong evidence about 85%, innocent people convicted under 5%).
