Task 35 design reply is on main: docs/ONE_CHARACTER_PLAN.md (docs only, no game code changed; I will not build it until the owner decides). Summary:
1. Going to one character is mostly subtraction. All autonomous actions pass through two places: SimDirector._fill (which calls Autonomy.choose) and Folk.enqueue. About 50 non-UI enqueue sites in 25 files exist today; the plan blocks them for the pawn with a data allowlist (origin: choice | body | hazard | hold on interactions) and proves it with a 14-day leak test.
2. Measured by running Godot headless: a character left completely alone has a bladder accident at about 27 h, passes out at about 39 h and collapses from hunger at about 80 h (3.4 days); a housemate or an ambulance then rescues them (12 h hospital stay in the ambulance case). With all 3 folk neglected for 8 days: 6 collapses, 6 ambulance runs, nobody died. The existing body rules are the safety net, so no new survival AI is needed, only warnings (question 1).
3. Reflexes worth keeping: collapse, pass-out and accident (already in data), hospital and jail holds, the smoke-alarm wake, and fire/disaster flee after a pause plus a short grace window; a six-point test says what counts as a reflex. Choking, extinguisher, rescuing a baby, seeking shelter, eating and toilet are not reflexes.
4. Routines (question 2) are literal player-written rules, capped at 8 per pawn, each with one trigger, 1 to 3 steps, no "else", and a visible failure message instead of improvising; they run as logged commands with a player-origin flag. I think this is within the rule, but it is the owner's call.
5. Pacing (question 3): the minimum chores are about 10 orders per game day at today's rates. Tedium comes from waiting and walking, so the plan adds a solo auto-skip for sleep, work and long trips, reusing the co-op skip logic; the rate on the big map with crowds is an unknown (measurement plan and targets in the doc, plus a deterministic "pawn bot" test player).
6. Household (question 4): a request system (`ask` command, 14 requests in data); obey chance from liking, role and age, traits, mood, needs and nagging, deterministic and shown as words (likely, maybe, unlikely); NPCs offer suggestions, not actions; no switching: the folk row becomes a household strip and CommandActions._interact rejects orders to any folk that is not the pawn.
7. Co-op (5): each player has one pawn (the existing Players head); skip is decided by whether the pawn is passive; the existing autopilot (3 idle minutes count as passive) is dangerous without autonomy, so the plan adds an AFK hold that freezes the pawn safe (open question).
8. What changes (7): 25-plus systems, listed with file names and breakage in section 7 of the doc: director, DynLife/DynPlans/DynReact/DynSocial, PlaceAuto, BabyCare, Homemaker, AutoFurnish, Commute/CareerShifts/SkipRules, Storyteller, Succession/HeirLine, Tiers, Folk.decay_mult and Coop/HUD. The largest unknown is dice-based incidents where the pawn is the actor (crime force and revenge, pregnancy dice, adoption, move-ins): 6 to 10 sites expected, not yet verified one by one.
9. Tests: 345 test-like files, 117 drive the real clock or autonomy. With the mode defaulting to household none change (only start_difficulty_test, start_scenarios_test and start_kit touch New Game), and the hash baseline is unchanged in household mode (the old game stays bit-identical); a second baseline is added for one-character mode.
