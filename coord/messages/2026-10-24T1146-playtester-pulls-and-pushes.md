Owner question: in one game played as a random person, how often does the game pull the player into something, or push them somewhere they didn't choose? Build: main @ ce8c334, which includes the task 63 fixes.

The character was rolled with Random on every creator control. Abel, a Kitchen hand: guitar 1, charisma 1, perception 1, perks keen eye / strong / quick hands, flaws smoker / fear of heights. Solo Start, Normal, Classic storyteller, played 7 game days (day 1 08:00 to day 8 07:00) by script through the real HUD, answering prompts like a decent person. Every action start and every change of place was logged as either ordered by the player or not.

## Counts over 7 days

| What | How many | Per day |
|---|---|---|
| Questions the player didn't ask for (favour, 2 parties, someone moving in) | 4 | 0.6 |
| Questions the player started (asking staff about work) | 2 | - |
| Uninvited visits at home (neighbour twice, a friend once) | 3 | 0.4 |
| Moves the player didn't order | 10 | 1.4 |
| ... walking to work (commute) | 5 | |
| ... walking home after a shift, an interview or leaving early | 4 | |
| ... away at a party, after saying yes | 2 (one is the same move as a walk home) | |
| Actions started on their own (interview wait, go-here for commutes) | 13 | 1.9 |
| Toasts of any kind | 133 | 19 |

Every push I saw came from something the player set up: a job, an interview or a yes to a party. There was no random teleport, arrest or hospital trip in this game. Money went from 200 to 484 coins. The first offer came on day 2, at the first interview.

## What feels right
- Commutes are announced ahead ("Work at 10:00, Moss & Mug Cafe. You set off around 9:47.").
- The new interview scene reads well: "You're early. They'll see you at 12:00.", then "Kenji sits down with you for the interview.", then the offer 18 minutes later.
- Parties happen when they say: a yes at 13:35 to "a party this evening" left at 18:58.

## Pushes that surprised me (most important first)

1. **The job changed without asking.** The phone application from day 1 stayed open after the in-person hire. Day 2: hired as Line Cook, starting at 10:00. Day 3: "They'd like to meet you: tomorrow, 11:00" from the same cafe. Day 4: "The job is yours. You start tomorrow, 6:00", and the job became Barista. The player was never asked whether to leave Line Cook. Day 4 at 09:48, staff even offered "We need a barista" while that application was waiting ("You have already applied there."). Getting hired should withdraw other applications, or the second offer should ask "Take it and leave Line Cook?".
2. **Ordering food at your own workplace ends the shift.** Day 5, 10:36, on shift as Barista (shift starts 06:00): "Order a Meal" at the counter, and at 10:37 "Abel earned 41 coins at work (left early!)". There was no warning, and the shift paid 41 instead of about 118. A meal break shouldn't count as walking out, or it should ask first.
3. **Closing a question can say yes for you.** Day 8: "Someone needs a bed: Blythe Dawson has lost their home for a while and asked if they could stay with Abel." The prompt was closed with Close, not answered, and at 07:00 "Blythe Dawson moved in on the sofa for a couple of days." Close should mean "decide later" or "no", never "a stranger moves in".
4. **Walks home after an interview or a shift have no toast.** For example, day 2 after the offer at 12:18, Abel walked home at 12:19 on his own. Commutes to work are announced, but the walks home aren't. Small, but the player sees the character leave without being told why.
5. **"Nobody free to go (at work, school or a party)"** was the only answer to every walk order during a shift: 15 toasts on days 3, 4 and 7, six of them in one hour on day 7. It's written for a household, and it doesn't say how to leave work. In a one-person game it could say "You're at work until 13:00." and offer a way to leave early.

Not a push, but the volume is worth a note: 19 toasts a day, many about townies (a break-in at someone's home, a house fire a few streets away, cable faults, power cuts).
