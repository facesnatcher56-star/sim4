**21: A criminal life as a playable path, and security systems.** From the owner. It builds on 04, 09, 09b, 09c and 20. Follow 17's rules. It's game mechanics only: no real-world instructions, abstracted as skills, rolls, interactions and scenes.

**1. Playing a thief (or any criminal)**
- **A "criminal" path** the player's folk can choose (or drift into with desperation from 09c). It's a hidden career with ranks (petty thief, burglar, fence, crew boss, mastermind), reputation in the underworld, and contacts (townie criminals met at bars or through a record).
- **Skills:** a new `stealth` skill (lockpicking and sneaking abstracted), plus handiness (alarms and safes), logic (planning), charisma (cons and fencing) and fitness (escaping).
- **Jobs, from small to big:**
  - pickpocketing in crowds,
  - shoplifting,
  - car theft (11b),
  - burglary of homes,
  - store and business robbery,
  - scams and cons (09c),
  - **planned heists** on banks, jewellery stores or the museum (14), with **casing** the target (learn guard routes, cameras, the alarm), recruiting a crew (townies with skills, loyalty and a cut), gear, a getaway car, and loot.
- **Selling the goods:** stolen items must be **fenced** through a fence contact (a lower price, and a risk the item is traced), or kept hidden (police searches can find them).
- **Risk and consequences:** every action rolls against detection (witnesses, cameras, guards, alarms, police patrols, crime rate) and leaves evidence for 09b discovery. That leads to arrest, trial and sentence (20), with the record and the underworld reputation going in opposite directions. Crew members can snitch for a plea deal, and rivals can rat you out.
- **Moral weight:** victims are real townies with ledgers, grief and grudges (revenge chains, 09c). Family members may find out (secrets), and the legacy score has a "notoriety" side.
- **Townies** run the same path statistically. Other criminals are your rivals and occasional partners.

**2. Security systems** for homes and businesses
- **Objects:**
  - door locks (grades), window locks,
  - **burglar alarms** (with an alarm-company response, linked to the police),
  - **security cameras** (record a footage log: evidence that boosts identification and conviction, and that can be erased or dodged by skilled thieves),
  - **motion sensors**, **safes** (grades, hold valuables and cash),
  - security lights, a guard dog (pets, 14),
  - a **guard booth** and **security guard** staff.
- **Security guards:** a new career (or a business staff role) with patrol routes, shifts, alertness that drops when tired, and the chance to catch, chase or call the police. A **night watchman** works at closed businesses.
- **Who has what:** each business type has a security level by value:
  - **bank:** vault, guards, cameras, alarms;
  - **jewellery store and museum:** cameras, alarms, display cases;
  - **market:** cameras, a store detective;
  - **cafe:** little.
  
  Owners (player or townie, 13) can buy upgrades. The town budget (12) pays for police patrols.
- **Effects:** security lowers break-in odds and raises detection and evidence. Insurance discounts for alarms and cameras (13). False alarms happen now and then.

- Fencing traces stolen goods to the thief at the expected rate.
- A crew member snitches under a plea offer.
- A guard asleep on a night shift misses more.
- Townie crime rates stay rare overall (19 point 3) even with the criminal path enabled.
