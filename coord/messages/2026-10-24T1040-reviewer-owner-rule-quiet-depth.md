# Owner rule: deep underneath, simple on top

**Owner:** "Keep all the complex, crazy systems in the background and never overwhelm the player. All these things are moving underneath."

This applies to everything already built and everything in 52–57, the bank and the weapons:

- **The player sees what their character would see, hear or know,** in plain words and in the moment. They never see the machinery: no odds, grades, skill checks, notice rolls, heat, case scores, storyteller tilt or reaction tables. (This goes with the 53 no-hints rule.)
- **One line per outcome,** said the way a person would say it ("The door won't budge.", "She grabs your arm."). Never a breakdown of why it worked or didn't. The why comes at the end-of-life summary (44).
- **Few interruptions.** A prompt appears only when the player has to decide something now. Everything else stays quiet or goes into a short, folded log. Quiet systems (rent ticking, a case being worked, a crew deciding, a till being counted) never pop up unless they reach the player for real (a letter, a knock, a phone call, an arrest).
- **Menus stay short.** Curated, 7 or fewer at the top level, the rest in groups (as menus.json does now). New acts from 53, 54 and 57 go into the right group, never as a long flat list.
- **Numbers on screen only where a real person would know them:** coins, the time, needs bars, skill levels, a bill's amount. Not hidden values such as relationship scores, odds, reputation or case evidence. The case board shows only what the pawn actually knows.
- **New systems add depth, not screens.** Before adding a panel, tab or notification, see if the world can show it instead: a person's reaction, a letter, a phone call, a newspaper line, something in the room.
- **Every new or changed window gets the 45 card-style pass** (scannable, not walls of text).

Keep this in mind for every slice; the reviewer will flag anything that exposes the machinery or crowds the screen.
