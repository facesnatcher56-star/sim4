# 53 amended (owner): no hints at all. The world tells you, through people who are really there.

**Remove from 53 the whole "they should know something is wrong" section.** The owner was clear: no thought like "this isn't my car" or "this doesn't seem right", no hesitation beat, no guilty or uneasy buff on the attempt, no icon tint marking that it isn't theirs, and nothing vague. No foreshadowing of any kind. The player does what they want, and they find out only from how the world reacts.

(A pawn can still feel things afterwards because of what actually happened: shame after being caught, a thrill after getting away. That's a consequence, not a hint.)

**How the world tells you: people who are physically there and notice.** The owner's example:
- The cashier stands at a till near the entrance, where a real store puts one.
- The pawn picks up goods and heads for the door without paying.
- Near the exit, the cashier asks "Are you going to pay for that?"
- The pawn now gets real choices: pay (goes to the till), put it back, argue or lie, or keep walking or run.
- If they walk past the cashier and out, it plays out for real: shouting, a chase or a guard, a call to the police, a description and a case.
- If the cashier is busy, looking away or on a break, nobody asks. Whether anyone stops you depends on who is there and what they can see, not on the game deciding it.

**Apply the same logic everywhere.** Each place puts its people where they really are, and they react to what they can perceive (line of sight, hearing, distance, attention, as in 49).
- **Restaurant, bar, café:** the host or server notices you leaving without paying. A bouncer at the door checks ID and turns away the visibly drunk.
- **Bank:** a teller at the counter, a guard by the door. Going behind the counter or for the vault gets a challenge, then the alarm.
- **Office, job site:** the receptionist at the front asks who you're here to see. Staff-only doors have staff nearby who say "you can't be back here".
- **Police station, courthouse, city hall, hospital:** the front desk or security screening at the entrance. A nurse stops you entering a ward. A bailiff stops you approaching the bench.
- **Homes:** a resident who is home and sees or hears you reacts (49). Neighbours at windows or in yards notice someone at a door or breaking a window. Nobody home and nobody watching means nobody reacts, until it's found later.
- **Cars:** the owner nearby, passers-by, a car alarm (only if that car has one, which is random).
- **Parks, streets:** passers-by, a jogger, a patrol car react to a fight, someone lying in the road, or public urination, scaled by how many are around and who they are.
- **Your job:** a coworker or the boss who is actually on shift notices you sleeping at your desk or taking stock.

**Build notes:**
- Each civic or business interior from 46 needs its staff posts: the till by the entrance, the front desk, the door guard, the host stand. Staff stand at their posts while on shift.
- A shared "noticing" step (who can perceive this act right now) feeds one reaction picker: ask, warn, refuse, block, call it in, chase, fight, or ignore.
- Lines vary (48: never the same way twice in a row).
- Anything you are holding that hasn't been paid for counts as unpaid goods. That's the trigger, not a menu label.

The rest of 53 stands, as amended here.
